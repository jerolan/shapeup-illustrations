# Choose a visual argument

| Need | Composition | What must be visible |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Social tension or decision | One vignette with two to four simple people | The action, reaction, and source of disagreement |
| Change in behavior or understanding | Two or three panels with a stable scene | One meaningful difference between panels |
| Progression across an explanation | Recurring diagram or scene updated at successive points | Recognizable continuity, the changed state, and the direction of evolution |
| Parallel progress or a stalled part | Repeated snapshots with stable item identities | Which items move, which remain fixed, and what that difference means |
| An explanation that builds or revises an idea | The same artifact with successive additions, strikeouts, edits, and rerouted connections | The precise edit and a short message explaining its significance |
| A list gaining content, organization, or completion | Recognizable entries across successive list states | Which entries were added, edited, moved, canceled, or completed |
| Containment or scope | An irregular outline subdivided into named regions | What belongs together and where the boundary lies |
| Flow or interaction | Handwritten places, actions, and connecting arrows | Starting point, transition, and outcome |
| Integration | Two schematic layers joined by one common slice | The same completed portion through both layers |
| Hidden complexity | A visible surface and a larger concealed structure | Why apparent size differs from effort |
| Uncertainty becoming resolved | A path or conceptual hill with a few marked states | The transition from discovery to execution |
| Decision against a reference | A simple comparative diagram | What is compared and why it changes the decision |

These are grammar, not a requirement to repeat the book's metaphors. Choose a new object or situation when it explains the user's idea better.

## Article sets

Identify the article's distinct claims before choosing image count. For a planning request, provide a compact shot list: placement, claim, composition, action or relationship, visible text, and filename. For a generation request, use that list internally and proceed with generation. Respect an explicitly requested count.

Alternate vignettes and diagrams only when the content warrants it. Reuse the visual language of recurring concepts rather than inventing a new symbol each time. If successive paragraphs develop the same idea, consider updating one shared diagram across those placements; read [progression.md](progression.md) for the sequence contract.

## Concept examples

- A team committing too soon: people enthusiastically adding blocks to a proposal while another person indicates the remaining space in a small container.
- A handoff losing context: the same object passed across two panels, with a missing connecting piece made visible in the second.
- A policy with exceptions: one boundary enclosing the ordinary cases, with an explicitly labeled exception path.

The scene must communicate the supplied claim without implying new facts, measurements, or causality.
