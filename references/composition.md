# Choose the visual argument

A claim says what the reader should understand. The carrier is the object or
relationship through which they understand it. Choose the carrier for its ability
to expose the claim, rather than for familiarity or decorative appeal. Lists,
charts, partitions, puzzles, stacks and other metaphors are options, not presets.
Generic labeled boxes are useful only when their connections actually explain the
claim. Do not substitute a sequence of different solutions when the requested
progression is the evolution of one conceptual artifact.

## Single idea, one image

Use one action or spatial relationship: a boundary selects scope, a connection
bridges two parts, a contrast reveals a difference, or a hidden portion explains
why apparent size is misleading. Each mark should help make that one claim legible.
People are optional. Add labels only when the relationship would be ambiguous.

## Conceptual progression: one carrier evolves

Keep the same artifact recognizable while its state or the reader's understanding
changes. A progression can develop an argument without depicting elapsed time.
Plan a short internal table before generation:

| State | What remains | Visible operation | What this demonstrates |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Establish | Carrier and entity identities | Starting configuration | Initial understanding |
| Develop | Same frame, scale and unaffected content | Add, remove, regroup, partition, move or connect | New consequence or unresolved issue |
| Reassess | Same carrier and identity mapping | Next meaningful transformation | Supported conclusion or open question |

Use as many states as needed, not a mandatory three. Choose one principal operation
per transition. Preserve geometry and content unaffected by it. A removed item
must be absent from its original place; a changed connection must have different
endpoints; a completion mark must denote completed work described by the source.
Keep rejected alternatives legible when seeing the rejection matters. Distinguish
new entries, cancellations, moves and completions.

Useful mechanisms include movement along a stable path, accumulation of discoveries,
revision of a list, partition of an outline, integration across layers, and parallel
entities advancing or stalling. A familiar metaphor is not a substitute for showing
what the transformation means. A small change can reveal a dependency; adding more
finished pieces can leave the conclusion unchanged until the pieces connect.

For object metaphors, show the relevant relationship explicitly: support below a
load, adjacency of pieces, containment of a selected portion. Avoid invented
causality or impossible geometry. Charts used conceptually carry no unsupported
percentages, measured axes, forecasts or proportional data claims.

Use a strip or grid within one bitmap when one image is requested. In an article,
successive files can appear beside the paragraphs developing the idea. For separate
files, attach the first selected frame as continuity reference for later states;
inspect the sequence together. A request for progression is not a request for animation.
Use short notes only when needed to explain the implication; preserve requested text.

## Character narrative: people develop a shared idea

Repeat recognizable people, props and viewpoint while gesture, dialogue and the
shared artifact develop the situation. Give each panel an explanatory beat. A
proposal, objection, revision and trial can work; use the supplied story rather
than manufacturing agreement or completion to fit a template.

For four requested panels, use a 2×2 grid read top left, top right, bottom left,
bottom right. Other counts and layouts remain available. Use whitespace or light
hand-drawn dividers. Put dialogue inside its corresponding panel, with bubbles
clearly attached to the speaker. Keep it concise, faithful to the source, or
verbatim when exact strings are supplied. Outside captions cannot replace requested
in-panel text. The shared drawing must evolve too; unchanged scenes with different
speech do not establish progression.
