# Drawing language

## Line and space

Draw black pen or marker contours on clean white. Lines have natural wobble, rounded ends, and small variations in weight. Keep generous whitespace around a small number of distinct objects. Use flat, unfilled forms with clear silhouettes. The result should feel like a thoughtful sketch made during a conversation.

Use medium pen strokes for people and diagrams. Use a distinctly thicker marker for deliberately rough structural sketches. Keep this difference intentional; roughness expresses which details remain open.

## People and emotion

Use unfilled oval heads, simple outlined torsos, thin limbs, and small oval hands. Let posture, pointing, raised arms, leaning, sweat marks, and speech or thought bubbles carry emotion. Faces can remain blank. Give people a role in the explanation: proposing, questioning, discovering, negotiating, or demonstrating. They need not appear in every illustration.

Keep workplace props schematic: a table, a few cards, a whiteboard, or a laptop outline. A laptop may be an incidental prop; its screen remains blank or abstract. Product screens, browser chrome, dashboards, and screenshot overlays do not belong in the composition.

## Diagrams and metaphors

Use boundaries for containment, arrows for meaningful direction, dots for distinct items, and checkmarks for completed parts. Label relationships that would otherwise be ambiguous. Ground an analogy in the actual claim: a bridge connects something, a boundary excludes something, a hidden mass explains unseen effort.

For rough structural sketches, render unspecified copy as loose strokes and keep decisive labels readable. A hand-drawn interface concept is eligible; a realistic interface rendering is outside this style.

## Color and lettering

Black carries the structure. A restrained accent identifies a change, warning, correspondence, or emphasis. Multiple scopes can have distinct colors when those colors track their identity across a sequence; this overrides the usual one-or-two-accent default.

Use casual, readable handwritten lettering with ample room inside bubbles. Keep accents and punctuation in the user's language. Prioritize correct readable text over ornamental handwriting.

## Style boundaries

Keep the canvas free of screenshots, screenshot fragments, screenshot tracing, UI mockups, polished stock-vector people, 3D rendering, photorealism, gradients, dramatic lighting, heavy shading, decorative textures, brand marks, and watermarks. Build an original scene rather than a replica of a reference page.
